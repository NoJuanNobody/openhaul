What is OpenHaul and what is it like to use?

Long image

A

Answer

OpenHaul is the matching and custody engine for a planned delivery network where independent drivers carry parcels in their own vehicles. There is no app yet: today you run it as code and a simulation, not as a service a shipper or driver can sign up for.

  1. Every 2 seconds a dispatch loop pairs open delivery requests with free drivers who have room, using one Rust matching engine.1
  2. The driver sees only the next checkpoint; the real drop-off appears within 0.8 km of it.2
  3. Each job moves through custody states, and a cold-chain parcel gets a Groth16 proof that its temperatures stayed in band.3
  4. Pilots, real drivers, signed RFID scans and the legal entity do not exist yet.4

What happens to one parcel, from request to drop-off?

Diagram structure
  • Shipper shipper
  • Dispatch loop loop
  • Matching engine engine
  • Driver driver
  • Custody record custody
  • shipper → loop · Delivery request: 20 lb, 2–8 °C
  • loop → engine · Free drivers + open requests
  • engine → loop · Best match: driver 0.60 km away
  • loop → driver · Job + first checkpoint only
  • driver → custody · Pickup scan + band proof → in custody
  • loop → driver · Drop-off shown inside 0.8 km
  • driver → custody · Delivered → completed
  • custody → shipper · Custody history + proof
  1. Shipper ↗

    Posts a delivery request: pickup, drop-off, weight, volume, priority, deadline, payout and, for cold chain, a temperature band.

  2. Dispatch loop ↗

    Every 2.0 s it keeps only drivers who are online and have spare weight and volume.

  3. Matching engine ↗

    Scores each driver–request pair and picks non-overlapping matches, highest score first.

  4. Driver ↗

    Gets the job and sees one checkpoint at a time, not the final address.

  5. Custody record ↗

    Moves IDLE → EN_ROUTE → IN_CUSTODY_RFID_VERIFIED → COMPLETED, or DISPUTED_EXCURSION if the cold chain breaks. Entering custody needs the RFID scan and a proof that the readings stayed in band.

What did a real run of that example print?

We ran one tick in New Orleans with one clinic cooler request: 20 lb, 3 cu ft, 2–8 °C, medical-critical, $40 payout. Two cold-capable drivers were registered, and one was offline.

  • The offline driver was filtered out before matching, so only one driver was considered.
  • Score = $40 × 2.5 (medical-critical) − 0.60 km × 0.75 + 4.42 km × 0.2 = 100.4.5
  • The drop-off coordinates appeared only once the driver was 0.51 km away, inside the 0.8 km threshold.2

Who goes first when drivers are scarce?

The engine multiplies each payout by a priority weight, then subtracts 0.75 per km the driver must travel empty to reach pickup. Drivers more than 40 km from pickup are skipped.5

Priority tierPayout weight
Standard1.0×
Expedited1.5×
Medical critical2.5×
Disaster triage (FEMA tier)Name only6.0×

The FEMA tier is a priority label in code. OpenHaul has no FEMA or government contract.6
